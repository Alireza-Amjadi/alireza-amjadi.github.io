05Heat Transfer

Hybrid Cooling of Power Electronics

A transient finite-volume heat-conduction solver written from scratch in MATLAB, used to pass or fail a liquid–air cooled power-electronics block against its 75 °C limit.

ReportRepository
Course
Heat Transfer I
Term
Spring 2026
Team

Tools
  • MATLAB
  • ANSYS

Overview

Does the block, heated by a chip whose heat flux oscillates as q″(t) = 25 000 |sin(πt/300)| W/m² and cooled by a liquid channel and forced air, stay below 75 °C, and can the numerical answer be trusted?

Approach

  1. 30 × 15 cm alloy-steel block with a hot-chip cavity (oscillating heat flux) and a coolant cavity at fixed wall temperature.
  2. Conservative finite-volume discretization on a uniform grid; a mask matrix and face-type codes give one unified energy balance for every node type.
  3. Explicit (FTCS) solver with the stability limit evaluated over all nodes, cross-validated with an implicit (Backward-Euler) solver.
  4. Quasi-periodic steady state, a coolant-temperature study, grid and time-step independence, and validation with ANSYS Mechanical.
